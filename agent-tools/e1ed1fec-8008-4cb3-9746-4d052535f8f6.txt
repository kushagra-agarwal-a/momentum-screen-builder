@Zubin Daruwala I have backtested this kind of a strategy. Here are the challenges and what I observed after checking trades, etc:  
  
• Most of the ETFs were listed in the past few years; if you try to do it from 2011 or 2012, there aren't many ETFs to check out. Therefore, you must use total return indices as a proxy. This introduces another complication: we can't backtest using the actual traded price.  
• Most of the outperformance for this strategy came from allocating to foreign and commodity ETFs. If you remove them and only use sector plus broad market domestic ETFs or indices, then the strategy underperforms even Nifty  
• A 12\-month or average of 12\-9-6\-3, etc., didn't perform as well as the standard 3\-month Sharpe lookback. So shorter lookback vastly outperforms.  
• Outperformance vanishes post 3\-5 holdings  
• In actual trading, it is almost impossible to deploy huge capital because there is not enough liquidity to deploy 20-50L of capital, so eventually you will work against yourself

Tactical cash call  
Great question — applying **Tactical Asset Allocation (TAA)** or a **cash call overlay** on top of a **momentum strategy** adds a **macro-level risk control** that can improve downside protection and adaptability during market stress.  
Let’s break it down clearly.  
  
🧠 Goal  
You have a momentum-based strategy (e.g., rotating stocks or ETFs based on 6m/12m returns). Now you want to:  
• **Reduce risk** in bear markets  
• **Hold cash** or defensive assets (e.g., bonds, gold) when momentum fails  
• **Avoid large drawdowns** while maintaining trend-following exposure  
🧭 Step-by-Step: How to Add Tactical Asset Allocation / Cash Overlay  
  
✅ 1\. **Define a Market Regime Signal (Risk Indicator)**  
Pick one or more **macro or technical indicators** to determine when to go “risk-off”:  
🔹 Common TAA Regime Filters:  
 Indicator Risk-Off Signal **Moving Average (MA)** Index (e.g., S&P 500) is below its 200-day MA **Breadth** Fewer than X% of stocks are above their 200-day MA **Volatility (VIX)** VIX > 20 or rising rapidly **Rate of Change** Index 6m return < 0% **Composite Score** Combine above signals with weights You can also build a **custom regime model** using logistic regression or machine learning if needed.  
  
✅ 2\. **Define Allocation Shifts (What Happens When Risk Is High)**  
Once your risk signal is triggered, reallocate a portion of the portfolio:  
Common Actions:  
 Mode Allocation **Risk-On** 100% Momentum Portfolio (e.g., top 10 stocks/ETFs) **Risk-Off (partial)** 50% Momentum, 50% Cash or Treasuries **Risk-Off (full)** 100% Cash, Bonds, or Defensive Assets You can do **hard switches** or **smooth transitions** using signal strength (e.g., cash weight = 1 − regime\_score).  
  
✅ 3. **Blend With Momentum Portfolio**  
Apply the overlay _after_ your momentum stock/ETF selection:  

```
if market_signal == "risk_on":
    final_portfolio = momentum_weights
elif market_signal == "risk_off":
    final_portfolio = 0.5 * momentum_weights + 0.5 * cash_or_bonds
```

  
Or with a dynamic weight:  

```
cash_weight = max(0, 1 - regime_score)
momentum_weight = 1 - cash_weight
final_portfolio = momentum_weight * momentum_weights
```

  
📉 Example  
You rotate between 10 sector ETFs using 12m momentum. But if the S&P 500 is below its 200-day MA:  
• Hold 50% in **SHY** (1–3y Treasury ETF)  
• Allocate only 50% to top 5 momentum ETFs  
This reduces exposure during downtrends and preserves capital for re-entry.  
  
🧪 Bonus: Composite Risk Signal  
You can build a **score-based model**:  
Risk Score=0.5⋅MA Signal+0.3⋅VIX Signal+0.2⋅Breadth Signal\\text{Risk Score} = 0.5 \\cdot \\text{MA Signal} + 0.3 \\cdot \\text{VIX Signal} + 0.2 \\cdot \\text{Breadth Signal}Then map that to cash exposure:  
Cash Weight=min⁡(1,Risk Score)\\text{Cash Weight} = \\min(1, \\text{Risk Score})  
✅ Benefits  
 Advantage Explanation **Drawdown Control** Cuts exposure during crashes **Smoother Ride** Reduces volatility for investors **Psychological Benefit** Easier to stick with during bear markets **Cash Opportunity** Preserves capital to re-enter cheaply  
⚠️ Caveats  
• Poor signals can lead to **whipsaws**  
• Must avoid **overfitting** to past bear markets  
• Missing a **sharp rebound** can hurt returns

Dynamically switching between **6\-month** and **12\-month momentum lookbacks** can enhance your strategy by adapting to changing market regimes. The idea is to \*use the right tool for the right market environment\*—shorter-term signals in fast-moving markets and longer-term ones in stable, trending conditions.  
Here are some **advanced and practical strategies** to do that:  
  
1\. **Regime-Based Switching**  
Use **macro or technical indicators** to define market regimes, then switch the lookback accordingly.  
📈 Example: Trend-Based Regime Detection  
• **Condition**:  
 ◦ If S&P 500 > 200-day moving average → Bullish → Use 12\-month lookback.  
 ◦ If S&P 500 < 200-day moving average → Bearish/Volatile → Use 6\-month lookback.  
• **Why**: In trending markets, longer-term momentum dominates. In volatile/down markets, shorter-term momentum is more responsive.  
📉 Example: VIX Threshold  
• If **VIX > 20** → Market is volatile → Use 6\-month.  
• If **VIX ≤ 20** → Market is calm → Use 12\-month.  
2\. **Momentum Signal Strength Comparison**  
At each rebalance, compute both 6\-month and 12\-month momentum ranks:  
• Compare the **average rank correlation** or **Sharpe ratio** of each signal across your universe.  
• Choose the one with:  
 ◦ Higher average signal strength  
 ◦ Better dispersion (larger performance spread between top and bottom ranked stocks)  
• **Why**: Dynamically favors the more "informative" momentum signal in current conditions.  
3. **Machine Learning-Based Selection**  
Train a model (e.g., **XGBoost**, **Random Forest**) to predict which lookback (6m or 12m) leads to better future returns based on:  
• Market regime indicators (VIX, yield curve, volatility)  
• Macro factors (e.g., Fed rate, inflation trend)  
• Technical indicators (breadth, volume spikes, moving average slopes)  
Let the model **choose the lookback** per period (or even per asset).  
  
4\. **Hybrid Momentum Signal (Weighted Blend)**  
Instead of hard switching, use a **weighted average** where weights depend on volatility, trend strength, or other indicators.  
• Momentum=w×R6m+(1−w)×R12m\\text{Momentum} = w \\times R\_{6m} + (1 - w) \\times R\_{12m}Momentum=w×R6m​+(1−w)×R12m​Set **www** dynamically based on:  
 ◦ Rolling realized volatility (e.g., higher vol → higher weight on 6m)  
 ◦ Slope of market index MA (steep trend → higher weight on 12m)  
5\. **Cross-Sectional Performance Tracker**  
Track **rolling performance** of portfolios built with 6m vs 12m momentum:  
• If 6m\-based portfolio outperforms 12m over the last 3–6 months, switch to 6m, and vice versa.  
• Could be done using **cumulative return or Sharpe ratio differentials**.  
6. **Ensemble Learning (Meta-Model Switching)**  
Build two sub-strategies:  
• One using 6m momentum.  
• One using 12m momentum.  
Then create a **meta-strategy** that:  
• Allocates capital dynamically between the two based on past performance, signal quality, or reinforcement learning.  
This creates a **robust ensemble model** where allocation shifts instead of pure switching.  
  
7\. **Clustering-Based Approach**  
Use **unsupervised learning (e.g., K-means)** on market features (volatility, correlation, macro) to identify latent regimes.  
• Assign each cluster a preferred lookback based on backtested outcomes.  
• In live trading, assign the current regime to a cluster and follow the corresponding lookback rule.  
Final Tip: Always Backtest  
Before deploying dynamic switching, ensure:
